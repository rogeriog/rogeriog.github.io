SOAP in 3D

Smooth Overlap of Atomic Positions turns a local atomic environment into a fixed-length vector that does not care about rotation or atom ordering. This page builds the idea in the order the maths does: density → basis → power spectrum → compression.

1 · The neighbor density

Put the central atom at the origin. Every neighbor contributes a smooth Gaussian. The sum is a density field: no atom labels, no orientation, just “how much stuff is where”. Drag to rotate; scroll to zoom.

drag to rotate · scroll to zoom

2 · Expand the density

SOAP expands the density in a basis: a radial function for “how far” and a spherical harmonic for “which direction”. That gives coefficients cnlm. The basis is truncated at n_max radial functions and l_max angular channels — here 4 and 4.

ρi(r) = Σj exp(−|r − rij|² / 2σ²)
ρi(r) = Σnlm cnlm(i) gn(r) Ylm(r̂)
pnn′l(i) = Σm cnlm(i) cn′lm(i)*

The power spectrum removes the m index, and with it the orientation. What is left is invariant under rotation, translation and permutation of identical atoms.

3 · Spherical harmonics and where cnlm comes from

The density is a function of position. To turn it into numbers, SOAP projects it onto a basis: a radial function gn(r) that asks “at what distance?” and a spherical harmonic Ylm(θ,φ) that asks “in which angular shape?”. Their product is one basis function; integrating the density against it gives one coefficient cnlm.

Real spherical harmonics up to l=3. Red is positive, blue is negative; the number of angular lobes grows with l. Click a tile to inspect it.

For every radial channel n and angular channel (l,m) you get one complex number. The power spectrum multiplies pairs and sums over m:

pnn′l = Σm=-l..l cnlm cn′lm*
Why this removes orientation: rotating the environment mixes the m components among themselves but leaves the sum over m unchanged. That is the invariance SOAP is built on.

4 · Watch a coefficient being integrated

This is the step the formulas hide. For one (n, l, m), the page samples the density on a 3D grid, multiplies by the radial function and the spherical harmonic, and adds the contributions one by one. The running sum is cnlm. Press play to fill the whole coefficient table.

Try the sampling modes: a uniform grid is symmetric, so for l>0 positive and negative lobes cancel to almost zero. Random-sphere and atom-cloud samples break that symmetry and give a non-trivial coefficient.

Red points contribute positively, blue negatively. The integrand is ρ(r) · gn(r) · Ylm(r); the sum over samples times the cell volume is cnlm.

What is illustrative here: the grid integration uses a simple Gaussian radial basis to make the arithmetic visible. dscribe uses its own GTO basis and returns the final power spectrum; the idea is identical, the numbers here are not the dscribe values.

6 · Compression: from hundreds of numbers to a map

SOAP vectors are high-dimensional and grow with the number of species. The mu1nu1 scheme from Darby et al. compresses the species dimension: for 3 species it cuts 390 numbers to 240, and the length then scales linearly rather than quadratically with the number of elements.

–variance in 2D

80 random clusters (dimer / tetrahedral / octahedral templates with jitter), featurized at r_cut=4 Å, then projected to two principal components. The cluster structure survives the compression — that is what makes SOAP useful for clustering and for appending compact codes to a model.

Why compress at all? The uncompressed power spectrum scales as (n_species·n_max)(n_species·n_max+1)/2·(l_max+1). mu1nu1 keeps species identity but removes the explicit species-pair cross terms; mu2 goes further and drops species labels entirely.

7 · The dimensionality problem

The uncompressed power spectrum grows with the square of the number of species-resolved radial channels. On a three-element demo that is 390 numbers; on a general dataset it is a different beast. Drag the species count and watch the length.

–mu1nu1

Formulas: uncompressed = (S·n_max)(S·n_max+1)/2 · (l_max+1); mu1nu1 = n_max² · S · (l_max+1); mu2 = n_max(n_max+1)/2 · (l_max+1), independent of S. At S=20, uncompressed is 16 200 numbers, mu1nu1 is 1 600, mu2 is 50.

8 · What to remember

SOAP is a local fingerprint. It is invariant, fixed-length, and interpretable through its radial/angular channels. But it depends on r_cut, n_max, l_max, and on how species are weighted. Change the cutoff and you are describing a different environment.
