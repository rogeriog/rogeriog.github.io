About Me

I am a Postdoctoral Researcher at MODL, IMCN, UCLouvain (Belgium) in the group of Gian-Marco Rignanese, where I advance the group's computational infrastructure. I developed MatterVial, a hybrid framework for enhanced accuracy and explainable AI; created VibroML to automate structural remediation with machine-learned potentials; and co-developed MEIDNet, a multimodal generative AI framework for inverse materials design — alongside custom GNNs and agentic frameworks wired into high-throughput workflows.

I hold a PhD in Materials Science (UFRGS), where I researched lead-free perovskites for solar cells and LEDs using DFT and machine learning. During a doctoral exchange at UCLouvain I enhanced the MODNet machine-learning package with a novel featurizer that significantly boosted benchmark performance — a model later instrumental in discovering new perovskites in my thesis and in my first-author npj Computational Materials (2026) paper combining feature-based approaches, GNNs, and symbolic regression.

My path into computational materials science began with an undergraduate internship at the University of Toronto, followed by a Master's in Computational Modelling on electromagnetic solar-cell simulations. After my PhD, a postdoctoral fellowship at UFRGS took me into self-driving laboratories — automated robotic platforms coupled with ML and LLM agentic frameworks to steer the discovery and solution-based synthesis of optoelectronic materials. I'm passionate about scientific innovation through robust software engineering, complex research challenges, and team collaboration.
